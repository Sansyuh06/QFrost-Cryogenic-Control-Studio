# QWE-002: Layout-Inflation / SWAP Storm

## Overview
- **Identifier**: QWE-002
- **Title**: Layout-Inflation / SWAP Storm (Routing Explosion on Sparse Topologies)
- **Baseline Severity**: HIGH
- **CVSS for Quantum Formula**:
  $$\text{score} = \text{clamp}\left(1 + 9 \cdot \frac{\log_{10}(\text{max\_ratio})}{\log_{10}(\text{cap})}, 1.0, 10.0\right)$$
  - Headline metric: $\text{depth\_inflation\_ratio} = \frac{\text{transpiled\_2q\_depth}}{\text{original\_2q\_depth}}$
  - $\text{budget} = 2.0\times$ (transpiled 2q-depth $\ge 2\times$ original is unacceptably bloated)
  - $\text{cap} = 2.5$ ($2.5\times$ depth multiplier maps to score 10.0)
  - Severity: HIGH if transpiled 2q-depth $\ge 2.0\times$ original, CRITICAL if $\ge 4.0\times$, MEDIUM if $\ge 1.5\times$.

## What This Weakness Is
Quantum algorithms are frequently specified in logical abstract graphs with long-range or all-to-all entangling operations. Real superconducting hardware (e.g., IBM heavy-hexagonal lattices) or trapped-ion linear chains feature strictly constrained physical connectivity.

When non-adjacent physical qubits must execute a two-qubit gate (such as CNOT or CZ), compilers must synthesize a chain of SWAP gates to bring the logical qubits into physical adjacency. In native hardware decompositions:
$$\text{SWAP} \equiv 3 \times \text{CX}$$
A single distant two-qubit gate across a distance $L$ requires $2(L-1)$ SWAP gates, which translates to $6(L-1)$ additional CNOT operations. In an unoptimized routing pass ("SWAP storm"), the circuit's two-qubit gate count and critical-path depth explode.

## Why It Costs Money and Time on Quantum Hardware
- **Fidelity Destruction**: Two-qubit gates are the dominant source of coherent and incoherent error on quantum processors (typical error rates $5 \times 10^{-3}$ to $1 \times 10^{-2}$ per CX). A $3\times$ increase in 2q-depth drops overall circuit fidelity by an order of magnitude:
  $$\mathcal{F}_{\text{circuit}} \approx (1 - \varepsilon_{2q})^{N_{2q}}$$
- **Execution Time & Decoherence**: Two-qubit gates take 10× to 15× longer than single-qubit gates (~200 ns vs ~20 ns). Ballooning depth pushes total execution time into the $T_2^*$ dephasing envelope.
- **Wasted Shots**: The user pays for tens of thousands of shots on completely decohered, white-noise output distributions.

## The Quantum Exploit Path
1. Algorithm engineer connects distant logical qubits in a chain or tree ($q_0 \leftrightarrow q_n$).
2. The user submits the circuit to a compiler targeting a sparse coupling map (such as heavy-hex) using default or greedy layout heuristics.
3. The transpiler injects dozens of SWAP operations, tripling two-qubit depth.
4. Total execution blows through physical coherence thresholds without any warning at code-review time.

## Mitigations & Auto-Fix
1. **Initial Qubit Placement (SABRE Heuristic)**: Employ bidirectional search (SABRE) or graph-embedding to map frequently interacting logical qubits to high-degree physical neighbors.
2. **QFrost Triage Auto-Fix**: QFrost Triage automatically generates an optimized layout-mapped circuit saved to `qfrost-triage/out/patched_<hash>.py`, binding virtual qubits to physical indices that minimize SWAP overhead.
3. **Hardware-Aware Gate Synthesis**: Re-synthesize blocks of gates using pulse-level cross-resonance or KAK decomposition rather than raw SWAP insertion.
