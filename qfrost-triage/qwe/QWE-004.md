# QWE-004: Fridge-Roundtrip Deadlock

## Overview
- **Identifier**: QWE-004
- **Title**: Fridge-Roundtrip Deadlock (Mid-Circuit Classical Feedforward Latency Exceeds Coherence)
- **Baseline Severity**: CRITICAL
- **CVSS for Quantum Formula**:
  $$\text{score} = \text{clamp}\left(1 + 9 \cdot \frac{\log_{10}(\text{max\_ratio})}{\log_{10}(\text{cap})}, 1.0, 10.0\right)$$
  - Headline metric: $\text{roundtrip\_vs\_t2\_ratio} = \frac{T_{\text{roundtrip}}}{T_2^*}$ (or total branch latency vs coherence budget)
  - Modeled roundtrip control latency: $T_{\text{roundtrip}} = 1,200\text{ ns}$ (per branch)
  - Budget: $\text{budget} = 1.0\times$ (round-trip latency vs per-step idle budget)
  - $\text{cap} = 4.0$
  - Severity: CRITICAL if any `c_if` / classically-conditioned branch implies cumulative idle wait $> T_2^*$ ($50,000\text{ ns}$), or if roundtrip latency exceeds fast cryogenic budget by $\ge 3\times$.

## What This Weakness Is
Dynamic quantum circuits introduce mid-circuit measurements and real-time classically conditioned branching (`c_if`, OpenQASM 3 `if-else`). In naive software simulators, classical conditional logic executes instantaneously ($0\text{ ns}$).

On real cryogenic QPUs (superconducting dilution refrigerators at 15 mK):
1. State readout requires dispersive resonator ring-up and cavity transmission ($T_{\text{RO}} \approx 300\text{ ns}$).
2. Raw analog pulses must travel up coaxial cables from the mixing chamber stage (15 mK) through intermediate temperature plates (4K, 50K) to room temperature (300K) ($T_{\text{cable}} \approx 240\text{ ns}$).
3. Room-temperature ADCs, FPGAs, and microcontrollers must demodulate the tone, integrate the signal, classify the qubit state, and execute the boolean branch instruction ($T_{\text{FPGA}} \approx 400 - 800\text{ ns}$).
4. The synthesized control pulse must propagate back down the cryostat lines to trigger the conditional gate ($T_{\text{down}} \approx 240\text{ ns}$).

Total round-trip latency:
$$T_{\text{roundtrip}} \approx 1,200\text{ ns} - 2,000\text{ ns}$$
During this entire latency period, all unmeasured data qubits sit idling in the cryostat, accumulating phase noise and dephasing according to $e^{-t / T_2^*}$. When circuits chain multiple mid-circuit feedforwards, data qubits completely dephase before the final conditional operation executes.

## Why It Costs Money and Time on Quantum Hardware
- **Silent Fidelity Collapse**: The circuit compiles and executes without throwing a runtime exception. However, every shot returns random garbage because data qubits decohered during classical loop arbitration.
- **Expensive Hardware Runs**: Real-time dynamic circuits require premium QPU backends (e.g., IBM Dynamic Circuit QPUs, Quantinuum H-Series), which command top-tier pricing tiers.

## The Quantum Exploit Path
1. Circuit includes mid-circuit measurements followed immediately by `c_if` conditional gates on data qubits.
2. In-flight data qubits hold coherent superposition without dynamical decoupling sequences.
3. The classical control system incurs hundreds to thousands of nanoseconds of signal propagation and FPGA processing.
4. Coherent state collapses due to $T_2^*$ decay before the conditional gate acts on the qubit.

## Mitigations
1. **Cryogenic Control Co-Processors**: Deploy Cryo-CMOS or 4K FPGA controllers inside the cryostat to reduce cable round-trip latency from $>1,200\text{ ns}$ to $<100\text{ ns}$.
2. **Dynamical Decoupling (DD)**: Insert Hahn echo ($X - X$) or XY4 pulse sequences on idling qubits during the classical roundtrip wait window.
3. **Deferred Measurement**: Replace mid-circuit feedforward with coherent quantum operations (e.g., Toffoli / controlled unitary gates) where circuit depth permits.
