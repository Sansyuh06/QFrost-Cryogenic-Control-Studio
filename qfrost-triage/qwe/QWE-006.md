# QWE-006: Vanishing-Gradient Ansatz

## Overview
- **Identifier**: QWE-006
- **Title**: Vanishing-Gradient Ansatz (Barren Plateau via Global Observables)
- **Baseline Severity**: HIGH
- **CVSS for Quantum Formula**:
  $$\text{score} = \text{clamp}\left(1 + 9 \cdot \frac{\log_{10}(\text{max\_ratio})}{\log_{10}(\text{cap})}, 1.0, 10.0\right)$$
  - Headline metric: $\text{shot\_explosion\_ratio} = \frac{2^N}{N_{\text{local\_baseline}}}$ (expected shot inflation to resolve gradient)
  - Cap: $\text{cap} = 64.0$ ($64\times$ shot inflation maps to score 10.0)
  - Severity: HIGH if parameterized two-qubit layers are present AND measured observable/register is global ($N \ge 6$ qubits), CRITICAL if $N \ge 12$, MEDIUM if $N \in [3, 5]$.

## What This Weakness Is
In Variational Quantum Algorithms (VQE, Quantum Neural Networks, QAOA), classical optimizers evaluate gradients $\partial_\theta \langle O \rangle$ of parameterized circuits $U(\theta)$.

When an ansatz includes alternating layers of single-qubit rotations and entangling gates (forming an approximate unitary 2-design) and evaluates a **global cost function** (an observable supported across all or most qubits, such as $O = |0\dots 0\rangle\langle 0\dots 0|$ or $Z^{\otimes N}$):
$$\mathrm{Var}_{\theta}\left[\frac{\partial \langle O \rangle}{\partial \theta_k}\right] \in \mathcal{O}\left(2^{-N}\right)$$
The variance of the gradient vanishes exponentially with the number of qubits $N$.

To resolve a gradient direction distinct from zero-mean finite-sampling noise, the number of measurement shots required scales exponentially:
$$N_{\text{shots}} \ge \Omega\left(\frac{2^N}{\varepsilon^2}\right)$$

## Why It Costs Money and Time on Quantum Hardware
- **Infinite Training Loops**: The classical optimizer observes gradients identical to zero across parameter space. The algorithm runs hundreds of iterations, sampling millions of shots per step, without moving towards a variational minimum.
- **Wasted QPU Budgets**: Variational runs often consume hours of continuous QPU access, generating substantial cloud invoices for completely stationary parameter trajectories.

## The Quantum Exploit Path
1. Algorithm employs a deep hardware-efficient ansatz (HEA) with parameterized rotations and entangling gates across $N \ge 6$ qubits.
2. The user measures all qubits simultaneously to estimate a global parity or projector cost function.
3. Gradient variance collapses below $10^{-3}$, requiring $>10^5$ shots per parameter step.
4. The optimization fails to converge while burning through allotted QPU execution credits.

## Mitigations
1. **Local Cost Functions**: Replace global observables with sums of few-body local observables:
   $$O_{\text{local}} = \sum_{j} Z_j \quad \text{or} \quad \sum_{\langle j, k \rangle} Z_j Z_k$$
   Local cost functions exhibit polynomially bounded gradients $\mathcal{O}(1/\text{poly}(N))$ for shallow circuits (Cerezo et al. 2021).
2. **Layer-by-Layer Initialization**: Initialize parameters using identity blocks or shallow perturbation layers rather than random Haar initialization.
3. **Problem-Inspired Ansätze**: Use symmetry-preserving ansätze (such as UCCSD in quantum chemistry) that do not sample randomly across the full Hilbert space.
