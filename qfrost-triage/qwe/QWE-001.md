# QWE-001: Unbounded Cut Overhead

## Overview
- **Identifier**: QWE-001
- **Title**: Unbounded Cut Overhead (Exponential Quasi-Probability Explosion)
- **Baseline Severity**: CRITICAL
- **CVSS for Quantum Formula**:
  $$\text{score} = \text{clamp}\left(1 + 9 \cdot \frac{\log_{10}(\text{max\_ratio})}{\log_{10}(\text{cap})}, 1.0, 10.0\right)$$
  - Headline metric: $\text{shot\_inflation\_ratio} = 4^k / \text{budget}$ (where $k$ is the number of cut two-qubit gates)
  - $\text{budget} = 16\times$
  - $\text{cap} = 16.0$ ($256\times$ shot inflation maps to score 10.0)
  - Severity: CRITICAL if $4^k \ge 64\times$ ($k \ge 3$), HIGH if $4^k \ge 16\times$ ($k = 2$), MEDIUM if $k = 1$ ($4\times$).

## What This Weakness Is
Circuit cutting (or circuit knitting) decomposes a wide or dense quantum circuit into smaller subcircuits executable on smaller QPUs or fragmented hardware. Cutting a two-qubit gate (e.g. CNOT or CZ) requires replacing the non-local operation with a quasi-probability expansion over single-qubit Pauli operations.

Reconstructing expectation values of the original circuit requires sampling across the tensor products of these operations, resulting in an exact shot multiplication penalty:
$$N_{\text{shots}} \ge \varepsilon^{-2} \gamma^2 = \varepsilon^{-2} \cdot 4^k$$
where $k$ is the minimum bisection cut size across the two-qubit interaction graph.

## Why It Costs Money and Time on Quantum Hardware
Cloud quantum providers (IonQ, IBM Quantum, Rigetti, AWS Braket) bill primarily on a per-shot basis:
- A circuit needing 4,000 shots on an integrated device will require $4,000 \times 4^k$ shots when cut across $k$ gates.
- For $k = 4$, shot inflation is $4^4 = 256\times$, turning a \$6.40 run (at \$0.0016/shot) into a **\$1,638.40 cloud invoice**.
- For $k = 6$, shot inflation is $4^6 = 4,096\times$, exhausting enterprise credits in minutes while yielding noisy classical post-processing reconstruction variance.

## The Quantum Exploit Path
1. An automated workflow partitions a quantum algorithm across multi-chip modules or smaller hardware targets without inspecting interaction cut-width.
2. Highly entangled subroutines (e.g., GHZ preparation, quantum Fourier transforms, or all-to-all variational layers) have high graph connectivity.
3. The compiler partitions the graph across a bottleneck requiring $k \ge 4$ gate cuts.
4. The client submits millions of classical sample requests to the QPU backend, incurring massive dollar burn without achieving quantum advantage.

## Mitigations
1. **Topological Co-design**: Constrain circuit partitioning algorithms with hard bisection cut budgets ($k \le 2$).
2. **Wire Cutting vs Gate Cutting**: Prefer wire cutting with state preparations and mid-circuit measurements where available ($3^k$ or $2^k$ overhead vs $4^k$).
3. **Entanglement Localization**: Reorder gates using SWAP routing to isolate entangled pairs onto single QPU clusters before invoking cut procedures.
